Mo3-L1: Key Concept Check

ตรวจสอบความเข้าใจหลัก (คำตอบแบบสั้น)

Directions: Answer each question on the lines provided.

1. How is a magnifying lens similar to the first microscope invented?
Both use a single lens to enlarge the appearance of an object. The first microscopes were essentially high-powered magnifying glasses (simple microscopes).
2. What did the invention of microscopes allow people to see?
It allowed people to see things that are too small to be seen with the naked eye, such as individual cells, bacteria, and tiny structures within organisms.
3. Leeuwenhoek's microscope could magnify an image about 270 times its original size. What kinds of objects did he observe with his microscope?
He observed "animalcules" in pond water (protists and bacteria), blood cells, and yeast.
4. In the 1700s, what important discovery about living things did Hooke make using his microscope?
He discovered that living things (starting with cork) are made of tiny, box-like structures which he named cells.
5. What can scientists today study about living things using microscopes?
Scientists can study cell division, the movement of live cells (using light microscopes), the internal organelles of cells (using TEM), and the detailed surface texture of microscopic structures (using SEM).

9. Compare a prokaryotic cell and a eukaryotic cell.
The prokaryotic cells are surrounded by a cell membrane but do not have a nucleus or other membrane-bound organelles. Their genetic material is not contained within a specific structure. The eukaryotic cells are generally larger and more complex, containing a membrane-bound nucleus that stores genetic material, along with many other specialized, membrane-bound organelles. Both types of cells are surrounded by a cell membrane and contain cytoplasm and ribosomes.
10. Use your responses above to summarize the main differences between a unicellular organism and a multicellular organism.
Unicellular organisms consist of a single cell that must carry out all life processes necessary for survival, such as growth and reproduction, within that one cell. These organisms can be either prokaryotic or eukaryotic. In contrast, multicellular organisms are always composed of many eukaryotic cells that work together. These cells undergo a process called differentiation to become specialized for specific tasks. This leads to a complex organization where cells form tissues, organs, and organ systems to ensure the survival of the organism.

Mo3-L4: Lesson Review Quiz (Short Answer)

แบบทดสอบท้ายบทเรียนที่ 4
1. List the four main types of tissues in humans. Then explain why the stomach has all four types of tissues.
Muscle, connective, nervous, and epithelial. The stomach contains all four because it must contract (muscle), be controlled (nervous), be held together (connective), and have a lining (epithelial).
2. List the three main types of tissues in plants. Then describe the function of each type of tissue.
Dermal (protection), vascular (transport), and ground (storage / support).
3. What do the stomach and a leaf have in common?
Both are organs made of different tissues working together for a particular job.
4. What do the human circulatory system and a plant's shoot system have in common?
Both are organ systems that work together to complete a series of tasks.
5. Why do multicellular organisms have more than one organ system?
Multicellular organisms have many systems to carry out all the various jobs needed for survival.
6. Compare the chromosomes in a skin cell to a muscle cell in the same organism. Include an explanation of how the two types of cells can be different.
A skin cell and muscle cell have the same chromosomes because they both come from one fertilized egg. They are different because of cell differentiation, where they become specialized for specific functions.

4. Explain the difference between diffusion and dynamic equilibrium using a perfume spray example.
Diffusion is when perfume particles first move from the high-concentration bottle into the lower-concentration air of the room. Dynamic equilibrium is reached when the perfume particles are spread evenly throughout the room; they still move, but the overall smell remains consistent everywhere.

Mo3-L6: Key Concept Check (Evaluate & Compare)

ประเมินและเปรียบเทียบแนวคิด
1. Compare diffusion and active transport in terms of energy use and direction of movement.
Diffusion is passive, moving particles from high to low concentration without energy. Active transport moves substances from low to high concentration and requires cellular energy.
2. Why is dynamic equilibrium important for homeostasis in the human body?
Dynamic equilibrium is vital for homeostasis because it allows cells to maintain a balanced internal environment (like stable oxygen or nutrient levels) even as particles constantly move in and out to support life processes.

Honey has a much higher viscosity (greater resistance to flow) than lemon juice, which slows particle movement and diffusion.
Oxygen moves quicker through air than water Medium
Air is a gas (molecules are far apart and move freely), whereas water is a liquid; diffusion always occurs fastest in gases.

Mo3-L7: Key Concept Check

คำถามอธิบายเหตุผล
1. Why does surface area make such a difference in dissolving medicine quickly?
A larger surface area means the substance has more contact with its environment. In the case of medicine, a larger surface area (like a powder vs. a solid pill) provides more "access points" for the liquid to interact with the particles, allowing it to dissolve quickly.
2. Why would a scientist want low viscosity for a substance that needs to diffuse quickly?
A scientist would want low viscosity because it allows substances to flow and spread out more easily. In a thinner liquid, particles can move more randomly and rapidly to fill a space evenly. This is similar to how smaller particles move through a membrane faster than larger ones because they face less interference. Ultimately, low viscosity ensures that a substance can travel down its concentration gradient quickly to reach dynamic equilibrium.

Mo3-L8: Key Concept Check (Short Answer — Explain with Evidence)

อธิบายปรากฏการณ์ในชีวิตประจำวันด้วยหลักฐานทางวิทยาศาสตร์
1. Why does a plant become wilted after applying too much fertilizer?
Fertilizer increases the solute concentration in the soil (making it hypertonic). Through osmosis, water moves out of the plant cells and into the soil to balance the concentration. This causes the cells to lose turgor pressure and shrink, resulting in wilting.
2. How is osmosis important when cooking with cucumbers or raisins?
When making pickles, salt draws water out of cucumbers (osmosis), making them crunchy. When soaking raisins in water, water moves into the raisin (osmosis) because the sugar inside is highly concentrated, causing the raisin to rehydrate and swell.
